# Restart Rituals After Crashes

For the days, weeks, or months when the system fell over. The comeback is the
skill.

## What a "crash" means here

A crash is any gap where the system stopped: three days on the couch, a
visitor-disrupted week, a depressive stretch, med changes, a new job, a
migraine, *life*. Every single person running any system at all crashes
sometimes. **The people with working systems are not the ones who never crash.
They're the ones whose restart costs almost nothing.**

That's the whole goal of this chapter: make the restart cost ~10 minutes, so
that "getting back on track" is a legit small task instead of a mountain.

## Why restarts fail (the three traps)

1. **The archaeology trap.** You look at the wreckage — 14 days of undone
   tasks, the laundry mountain — and start processing *all of it* before
   restarting. That's a huge task. You correctly nope out. Restart fails.
2. **The audit trap.** "Why did I fall apart? What's wrong with me?" Analysis
   paralysis. Post-mortems are for *good-energy* days, later, and only if they
   produce one change. Not for restart day.
3. **The revenge trap.** "This time I'll be PERFECT — bullet journal, 5 a.m.,
   gym, everything." You set a system sized like a hero day, at 30% battery.
   It dies in a day and the crash deepens.

The restart ritual avoids all three by having *one* first move, pre-decided.

## The Restart Ritual (10 minutes, in order)

Do this in order. Don't skip ahead to the fun part (number 4) — that's the
trap again.

1. **Say the landing words.** Out loud: *"I crashed. That's allowed. I'm
   restarting."* No job in this sentence: no analysis, no promises. Just
   landing gear.
2. **Declare amnesty.** Old tasks are dead, not owed. The list from before the
   crash is *gone*. What matters now is only what's true today. (Amnesty is a
   schedule decision, not a moral one — see below.)
3. **Reset the room base.** ONE ten-tidies-style action, today's energy level
   only: a bag of trash, the dishes *into* the machine (not washed), everything
   off the bed *onto* the chair. This is the "landing strip."
4. **Pick ONE anchor.** Tomorrow's single anchor task, priced for *today's
   battery*, not for guilt. Open your chore planner and enter exactly that,
   plus optional 2-minute slices. Done. Ritual over. That's the whole thing.
5. **(Optional, later, good day only): one retrospective question.** Not "what
   went wrong" — just: *"What cost more energy than usual?"* Write one line.
   That ledger entry is your best tool for next time.

Ten minutes. Anyone can spare ten minutes *on a crash day*, which is the size
requirement of a good restart.

## The Debt Question: is amnesty lying to yourself?

No — because debt logic is the audit trap wearing a costume. Ask instead:

- Which of these old tasks would still make my life **better today**?
- Which are dead because the moment passed? (Decluttering before the holidays
  is over. Old new-tabs. Old plans.)

The first, sorted by joy, is your *new* task list. The second gets deleted
*openly*, as a decision. Keeping dead tasks is how the list itself becomes
what you're avoiding.

## Rebuilding the streak

When restarting after long crashes, trust rebuilds through small counts, not
big days:

- **Minutes-only streak:** days you did *anything* count. A 2-minute version
  (see [Task Slicing](02-task-slicing.md)) keeps the streak alive.
- **Reputation, not perfection:** after 3 days of records, the system trusts
  again. Records are the whole game — see [Energy Accounting](01-energy-accounting.md).
- **First-week guardrail:** no anchor task may cost more than ~25% battery,
  and there must be a rest slot every single day for the first week.
