# Glossary

Plain-English definitions, friend voice. Words you'll see in this manual, in
the chore planner app, and in ADHD-adjacent corners of the internet.

## Terms

**2-minute version** — The smallest possible version of a task that still
counts as doing it. Exists specifically for bad-battery days.

**AI helper** — An AI chat assistant used here as a *planning clerk* (slicing,
pricing, drafting plans) or a *body double* — never as your boss.

**Amnesty** — Declaring an old backlog dead instead of owed. A schedule
decision, not a moral one. See [Restart Rituals](06-restart-rituals.md).

**Anchor task** — The one task that would make today a win. Sized to today's
battery, not to yesterday's ambition.

**Battery %** — Your subjective energy level, named like a phone's. 15% days
are real and allowed.

**Body double** — A person (or a silent AI chat) present while you work, doing
their own thing, providing presence but not help. See
[Body Doubling](03-body-doubling.md).

**Breadcrumb** — One line you write when parking a half-done task, telling
future-you exactly where to resume.

**Check-in timer** — A timer whose ring means "ask: in, park, or pivot?" —
never "you failed your 25 minutes." See
[Timers Without Shame](05-timers-without-shame.md).

**Cost (energy price)** — A number you guess (1–10) for what a task drains
*for you personally*. Avoided tasks usually have high prices you discovered
the hard way.

**Crash** — Any gap where the system stopped for a day or a hundred days.
A normal event with its own 10-minute fix (the restart ritual).

**Decision tax** — The invisible energy cost of every unmade choice. Task
slicing is mainly a way to pay this tax in advance, at full energy.

**Energy ledger** — The daily record of battery %, plan, and actuals. From
[Energy Accounting](01-energy-accounting.md) and
[Worksheet 1](07-worksheets.md#worksheet-1-the-daily-energy-ledger).

**Energy units** — The 1–10 price tags for tasks. Their only job is to make
plans that fit the battery you actually have.

**Guilt-free rest** — Rest taken deliberately, with your own permission and a
container (a timer and a one-word record), so it actually restores. Anything
you do to *avoid* the day doesn't qualify, however restful it pretended to be.
See [Guilt-Free Rest](04-guilt-free-rest.md).

**Local-first** — Software or files that live on your own machine and work
with no account, no subscription, and no internet. This manual, its site, and
its worksheets are local-first.

**Landing words** — The spoken restart sentence: "I crashed. That's allowed.
I'm restarting." Said out loud on purpose; it's the ritual's step 1.

**Overdraft** — Spending past your battery on a given day. Occasional overdraft
is human; three overdraft days in a row means the *routine* is mispriced (see
[Energy Accounting](01-energy-accounting.md)).

**Parked** — The honest third option when a timer rings: not "done", not
"failed", just paused leaving a breadcrumb. One of the three legal answers
(in, park, or pivot).

**Pre-sliced template** — A chore recipe already cut into slices, so future-you
doesn't pay the decision tax on the day.

**Rest slot** — A scheduled, named rest with your own permission in advance.
Rhythm rest (after each anchor task) and budget rest (more slots on
low-battery days) both count. See [Guilt-Free Rest](04-guilt-free-rest.md).

**Restart ritual** — The 10-minute, five-step comeback procedure after any
crash. See [Restart Rituals](06-restart-rituals.md).

**Restoration test** — "Do I feel *different* afterwards?" The test that
separates real rest from avoidance. See **Guilt-free rest**.

**Slice** — One 2–10 minute, decision-free, visibly-finished piece of a chore.
Plural: the only way big tasks get done around here.

**Streak (minutes-only)** — A streak where any recorded action counts, even a
2-minute version. Protects the system's trust during bad patches.

**Transition timer** — A 2–5 minute timer for the gap between doing and
resting (or back). The least famous kind, and secretly the most useful.

**Toll cost** — Extra energy spent on *starting* (finding supplies, changing
clothes). Doesn't show in the minutes, shows in the avoidance.
