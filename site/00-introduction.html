Introduction: Working With the Energy You Actually Have

Welcome. This manual is for days when everything feels heavier than it should.

If you have ADHD — or you're just chronically low on energy — you already know the cycle: a long list of chores, a body that says no, a brain that says later, and then the guilt. The guilt is the worst part. It follows you to bed.

This manual is a companion to our chore planner app. The app helps you split up chores and track what's done. This manual helps with the other half: the energy, the emotions, and the getting-back-up part.

What this manual will and won't do

It will:

  • Show you how to account for energy like a budget, so you stop overspending before noon.
  • Teach "task slicing" — cutting big jobs into slices you can actually chew.
  • Explain body doubling and why a second person (or an AI chat) makes tasks easier to start.
  • Give you full permission to rest — guilt-free, on purpose.
  • Offer timer techniques that create pressure without creating shame.
  • Provide a restart ritual for after crashes, bad weeks, or months off.
  • Show you how to use AI as a planning helper, with copy-paste prompts.

It won't:

  • Lecture you about discipline, willpower, or "just doing it."
  • Pretend every day has the same energy. It doesn't, and that's normal.
  • Give medical advice. This is practical education, not healthcare. Talk to your own clinician about anything medical.

The ground rules

  1. Low energy is information, not a character flaw. You plan around it the way you plan around rain.
  2. Done at half capacity still counts. A half-clean kitchen is a cleaner kitchen.
  3. The system serves you. If a rule in this manual consistently makes your life worse, delete the rule.
  4. Nothing here needs an account, a subscription, or a phone call. Everything is local-first: worksheets you can print, apps that run on your own machine.

How to use this manual

  • Read it in any order. Each chapter stands alone.
  • Fill in the worksheets in Worksheets — print them or copy them into a notebook.
  • Use the Glossary any time a word feels jargony.
  • If you only have five minutes: read Restart Rituals and Guilt-Free Rest. Those two carry the most weight on the hardest days.

A note on tone

You'll never see "just," "simple," or "everyone can do this" used to make you feel small. If any sentence here ever reads as condescending, that's our bug, not your failing. Tell us, and we'll fix it.

Now, take a breath. Let's start with the energy budget.
