Body Doubling

One of ADHD's strangest, best-documented tricks, explained and set up in about five minutes.

What it is

Body doubling is doing a task while another being is present, doing something — anything — alongside you. They don't help. They don't even need to do the same task. A partner reading on the couch, a friend on a video call quietly typing, a stranger in a focus livestream. All of these count.

Why it works (plain-English version): starting a boring task is the hard part, and-alone-ness makes starting feel optional. When someone else is present, their gentle attention holds the task open for you. You're not doing it because you're forced to — you're doing it because the room is already "task-shaped." For many ADHD brains, this turns starting from an act of will into something more like joining in.

This is not a character flaw. It's wiring. Heroes have spotter crews; nobody calls a powerlifter weak for having one.

The three levels

Level 1: The person next to you

  • Ask a partner, roommate, or kid: "Can you sit in the kitchen and read/paint/ doomscroll while I do the dishes?" That's the whole ask. No help needed.
  • Match rooms: they read on the couch while you tidy the living room.

Level 2: A remote double

  • Video call a friend with a rule: cameras on, mics muted, work in silence, check in for 60 seconds at the end. Several short calls beat one long one.
  • Focus-streaming sites: thousands of strangers quietly working together at set times. Pick a session that starts soon — a future start time is just a new way to procrastinate.
  • Dedicated virtual-body-double services and co-working-on-camera communities exist; most have free tiers. Beware any that require heavy sign-up ritual.

Level 3: The AI double

This is the level most manuals miss, and the reason this chapter is in a book about AI. An AI chat can behave exactly like a body double, because what matters is presence and gentle witness, not sentience:

  • Tell it: "I'm going to do the dishes for 10 minutes. Be my body double: stay quiet until time's up."
  • Or work in visible rounds: tell it the task, do it, come back and report. Its reply is a witness stamp — the digital equivalent of a nod from the couch.
  • It never sighs, never judges how long it took, and is available at 2 a.m. at no cost. Full prompts in Your AI Planning Helper.

An AI double is worse than a human double for the truly stuck moments, but infinitely better than no double at all. The ladder: human > remote human > AI > alone-with-a-plan. Use whatever rung is reachable today.

Etiquette and failure modes

  • Silence is the feature. If your double narrates, coaches, or checks in mid-task, it stops being a double and becomes a supervisor.
  • Don't save up tasks for a "proper" double. A double for 10 minutes of trash round-up is a correct use of a double.
  • Reciprocity, if you want it: offer your presence back. Sitting quietly while a friend does their dreaded task builds the mutual aid that keeps both of you going.

A one-line script to give people

"You don't have to help or even pay attention — just be in the room doing your thing. Weirdly, it makes my brain work. ADHD thing. Want to try?"

People who love you find this endearing. Ask anyway, even if it feels like a strange request. It's one of the cheapest, least demanding favors you will ever request, and for many people it's the single highest-leverage change in this whole manual.
